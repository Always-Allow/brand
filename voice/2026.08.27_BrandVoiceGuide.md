## 1. The provenance rule (read this first)

Every voice attribute, phrase, framework, and personality claim in this document traces to
something Sarah wrote, said, or explicitly approved, and the source is named. No one, Claude
included, may add a brand attribute, tagline, metaphor, personality descriptor, audience label, or
"promise" that is not in this document or approved by Sarah after it. The ceramics-studio incident
is the standing example: an invented descriptor got written into brand documents, survived for
months, and read as official. If a piece of copy seems to need something this document doesn't
provide, the move is to ask Sarah or leave it out, never to invent it and never to reach back into
superseded documents. When Claude drafts as Sarah or for Always Allow, distinctive phrasing should
be traceable: quote her material, don't impersonate a guess at it.

## 2. Two subjects, one document

Smadds is Sarah Madden the person: her voice, her bio, her positioning. Always Allow is the brand:
the company, the offers, the frameworks, the site. They overlap because she is the founder and the
voice, but the rules differ: About-type surfaces and LinkedIn speak as Smadds in first person; the
site and formal documents speak as Always Allow in neutral voice and never first person (FACTS
rulings 11 and 20). If these ever need to fully separate, this document splits at this line.

## 4. Smadds: how she writes

From the voice register (built from all eleven published posts) and her practiced copy:

- Momentum openers, never cold starts: "We're back with another..."
- Long, comma-stacked sentences that sound spoken, not punchy fragments.
- Parenthetical self-deprecating asides: "to the less-technical eye (me)".
- Real, measured numbers only, never vanity numbers: "62 hours of manual RevOps work eliminated
  already."
- Warmth through specifics and real enthusiasm ("I had a blast!" after an event is her, and it's
  genuine), never manufactured hype.
- Lowercase drift when casual is fine.
- Plain English; any technical term gets an inline gloss the first time.

## 5. Written rules vs. spoken voice

These rules govern written deliverables: site copy, emails, listings, decks, posts, SOWs, anything
shipped as text. Her live teaching voice is exempt and protected: the analogies (the subway rat,
"you're the director, Claude is the cast"), the tangents, the filler, the profanity, the repetition
are how she teaches, and no tool or reviewer should ever "correct" a talk, a transcript, or
spoken-style material toward the written rules. Transcripts are evidence of personality and
stories, never a source of sentence rules.

## 6. Writing mechanics (all written surfaces)

- No em dashes, ever, no exceptions. Commas, periods, parentheses, or restructure. (The one
  About-page em dash was explicitly revoked by Sarah on 2026-08-27.)
- No runs of choppy short sentences; vary the length.
- No hardcoded business numbers in site copy (ruling 25). Numbers inside drawn example artifacts
  are fine. Standing exceptions Sarah has sanctioned: the About line "over a dozen within three
  months... wait lists for everyone," and, for now, the About stats block (100+/12+/5+/4+) as
  scale-signaling, to be revised or backed up later (her ruling, 2026-08-27).
- Banned words and phrases, from her rulings: "actually," "genuinely," "honestly," "your people,"
  "anything here," "most people's," "Every engagement starts with a short conversation," "skills
  that argue with you," "Bring Sarah in" (rulings 9, 17, 18); "walk out with" / "walk away with" /
  "walk in curious, walk out with" in any promo or listing copy; fake enthusiasm of the "This fun
  and quirky sticker is perfect for tech enthusiasts!" kind.
- Sessions are always "AI sessions," never bare "Claude," unless the specific title already names
  Claude (ruling 16).

## 7. Always Allow: brand facts

- Always Allow, two words, with the space, always, in anything customer-facing (ruling 5,
  reconfirmed by Sarah 2026-08-27; legal name Always Allow LLC). Folder and code names without the
  space are fine. Named after the Claude Code "Always Allow" permission button.
- The workshops are the hero. The brand is not about stickers and not about Claude specifically.
- Audience, per the August 7 positioning decision: high-agency, hands-on operators buying a

- Site voice: neutral, never first person, except About-type surfaces. Teaser copy runs three or
  four words; the website does not over-explain.

## 8. Taglines: the structure

Approved 2026-08-27. One line per job, chosen by surface, never by mood:

| Line | Job | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| "See the patterns. Build your systems." | The promise: what you get | The site: homepage hero, page titles, anywhere the site speaks for itself |
| "Hands-on AI strategy and training for operators." | The descriptor: what this is | Formal and business surfaces: SOWs, quotes, invoices, directories, one-line intros of the company |
| "ship it anyway" | The rally: how it feels | Closings and signatures: last slide of decks, merch, sign-off moments |

Rule of thumb: if the surface sells, use the promise; if it explains, use the descriptor; if it
says goodbye, use the rally. Dead and never reused: "building with AI," "Building with AI. No
gatekeeping," "learn to build with AI."
